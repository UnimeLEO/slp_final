Plain Language Statement

You are invited to take part in a short study on how people make requests and negotiate in English through online text chat. Please read this statement carefully before deciding whether to take part.

What is this study about?

This study explores how users of English as a second language make requests and negotiate solutions in online text chat. We are interested in how people explain a problem, ask for a change of plans, respond to the other person's concerns, and reach an agreement. The study is not a test of your English, and there are no right or wrong answers.

Why have I been invited?

You have been invited because you are an adult (18 years or older) who uses English as a second or additional language. You should also feel comfortable reading short instructions and chatting by text in English.

What will I be asked to do?

  • Complete two separate role-play conversations in English by text chat. Before each round, you will receive a short situation card describing your role and what you want to achieve.
  • Chat with your partner until the conversation comes to a natural end. Each round usually takes about 15 minutes.
  • Complete a short questionnaire about your background and your experience of the tasks.

The whole session takes about 40 minutes in total.

What happens during the session?

  1. You read this Plain Language Statement and the Consent Form, and ask any questions you have.
  2. If you agree to take part, you electronically confirm the Consent Form.
  3. You receive the task instructions and situation card for the first round.
  4. You complete the first role-play conversation.
  5. You receive the situation card for the second round and complete the second conversation.
  6. You complete the short questionnaire.
  7. The researcher debriefs you, explains the study in more detail, tells you what type of partner you had in each round, and answers your questions.

What information will be collected?

  • Chat messages: messages you send and receive during the two role-plays.
  • Timing: the time each message is sent.
  • Typing and revision records: text you type, delete or change in the chat box before sending, including text you finally decide not to send.
  • Keystroke events: individual key presses and the time between them, recorded only inside the task's chat box.
  • Questionnaire answers: your background information and your views on the tasks.

No keystrokes are recorded outside the task's chat box, and no passwords or other personal accounts are accessed.

How will my information be used and protected?

Your data will be used only for a research report in LING90034 Second Language Pragmatics. It may also be discussed in class presentations for this subject.

  • Participant codes: You will be given a participant code. Your name will not appear in the data files or the report.
  • Storage: Consent records will be stored separately from the research data. All files will be kept securely.
  • Access: Only the researcher and the subject coordinator or assessors will have access to the data.
  • Retention: Data will be kept until final results for the subject are released and then securely deleted.
  • Quotations: Short, de-identified excerpts from the chats may be quoted in the report. Names, places and any other identifying details will be removed or changed. Text you typed but did not send may also be quoted briefly, but only after it has been anonymised.

Do I have to take part? Can I withdraw?

Taking part is completely voluntary. You can decide not to take part, or stop at any point during the session, without giving a reason. Your decision will not affect your relationship with the researcher, your studies, or the University of Melbourne.

If you stop during the session, any data collected up to that point will be deleted unless you agree that it may be kept.

After the session, you can withdraw your data by emailing the researcher with your participant code before 0:00 (GMT+11) on November 1, 2026. After this date, your data will have been de-identified and combined with other participants' data for analysis, so it may no longer be possible to remove it.

Are there any risks or discomforts?

The risks of this study are low. Negotiating a change of plans with someone who is reluctant to agree may feel slightly stressful. You can take your time and stop at any point.

Because typing and revision records are collected, text you type and then delete, or decide not to send, will still be recorded. Please keep this in mind while typing, and avoid entering personal information you do not want recorded.

If you feel uncomfortable during or after the session, you can tell the researcher at any time.
